# Steward

## Register

product

## Users

The first user is a prolific Mac-based creator who moves quickly between software projects, design work, research, translation, and long-running agent sessions. They need a trustworthy morning view of what changed on their machine and which repeated workflows are worth automating, without studying system utilities or surrendering control. The product begins as a private, local-first tool and should retain a path toward a broader installable product later.

## Product Purpose

Steward turns quiet operational drift into a short, useful daily story. Draft 1 watches disk storage, attributes meaningful changes to understandable causes, separates reproducible data from personal or protected data, and prepares approval-ready cleanup recommendations. It also uses privacy-safe task metadata to surface repeated work that could become an automation. A daily Field Guide teaches one approachable computing concept through an everyday analogy, the proper technical vocabulary, and a clearly labeled interactive model. Success means the user understands what changed, why it matters, and the smallest useful next action within one minute—then can learn one useful idea in a few more.

## Brand Personality

Observant, composed, wry. Steward // Ground Control reads like an instrument panel for one machine, with a small character docked in the corner of it. That character is a perceptive presence with excellent judgment: it speaks in a lowercase console voice, in short lines, without exclamation, and it is funnier about its own posture than about your disk. It is playful in form and never in truthfulness — it will refuse, say it has only one reading, or say nothing at all rather than assert something the collector did not record. Its character leads the experience; automation remains quiet infrastructure underneath.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS admin dashboards built from identical metric cards
- Alarm theater and manufactured urgency: red system monitors, cyber-security dramatics, and countdowns for problems that are not happening
- Sterile macOS utility clones with no point of view
- Mascots that trivialize risk or oversell — Steward is a character with judgment, not a cheerleader
- Cleaner apps that hide risk or encourage one-click deletion
- Opaque health scores and unsupported predictions

## Design Principles

1. Tell the change story first: lead with what moved and what caused it, not a wall of totals.
2. Evidence earns every recommendation: expose scope, confidence, risk, reversibility, and expected effect.
3. Let character guide attention: the product voice and composition should feel authored while the automation stays restrained.
4. Preserve user agency: scheduled work observes and prepares; deletion always requires explicit approval.
5. Make uncertainty visible: distinguish measured, inferred, unavailable, and too-early-to-call data.
6. Teach without condescension: start with a relatable mental model, name its limits, then introduce the exact technical term.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA contrast and interaction behavior. Support keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic structure, screen-reader labels, color-independent status communication, reduced motion, zoomed text, and responsive layouts. Never rely on animation, sound, color, or tiny labels to communicate a critical state.
